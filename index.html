No Names Protected

No Names Protected is a hip-hop album built around the moment forgiveness, discretion and repeated chances finally run out. It follows a man who spent years protecting people, keeping secrets and choosing silence, only to realize that his kindness, loyalty and privacy were being mistaken for weakness.

The record moves through betrayal, manipulation, broken promises, hypocrisy, toxic relationships, anger and accountability. Instead of hiding what happened, the songs confront the consequences directly. The album is not simply about rage; it is about what happens when someone stops protecting everybody else from the truth and begins protecting his own peace.

Across the project, the darker side of the story becomes more visible. Memories, receipts and unresolved experiences surface track by track until there is nothing left to hide. The central idea is transformation: the good in him is still there, but repeated betrayal has forced him to confront the darker version of himself that survived it.

“Some people bring out the best in you… others force you to let the darker you survive.”

The Story of Youngoztheking

Youngoztheking is the artist identity of Noel C. Osborne Jr., an independent singer, rapper, songwriter and performer from Edgewood, Maryland. His relationship with writing began around age twelve after the death of his brother. What started as a way to put grief, thoughts and emotions into words gradually became something much larger: a creative language for documenting his life.

Before building a public music catalog, performance was already part of his foundation. Dance, track and years of public speaking helped shape his confidence, movement and presence. That background still influences the way he approaches music today. A Youngoztheking record is not imagined only as something to hear; it can become choreography, a visual, a character, a scene or an entire era.

In 2019, he left home and entered the U.S. Army. The years that followed took him far beyond Maryland, including assignments in the United States and Germany and experiences across Europe. Military life brought discipline, responsibility, travel, pressure and personal change. Those experiences became another layer of the person behind the music, while songwriting remained a place where he could be vulnerable, reflective, romantic, angry, ambitious or completely unfiltered.

Youngoztheking refuses to build his identity around a single sound. R&B is a major foundation, but his catalog moves through Hip-Hop, Afrobeats, Dancehall, Latin influences, Drill and Jersey Club. That range is intentional. Different emotions require different production, different flows and different energy. Some records explore love, reassurance and intimacy; others confront heartbreak, trauma, betrayal, accountability, ambition or rage. The sound can change while the perspective remains his own.

His first live performance came in Germany in August 2025, marking another transition from creating music privately and releasing records online to presenting the work in front of an audience. Since then, the vision has continued to expand through albums, EPs, singles, lyric videos, official videos and an increasingly large visual catalog.

Projects such as No Holding Back, Love & Trauma, One Sided Love and Island Royalty represent different sides of that evolution. Rather than treating every release as an isolated attempt at a hit, Youngoztheking approaches music as a long-term body of work. Songs connect to larger themes, projects document particular periods, and visuals give those periods their own identity.

That philosophy is at the center of the brand: longevity over a temporary moment. The objective is to keep writing, experimenting, performing and building a catalog that listeners can discover years from now and follow from its earliest chapters forward. Youngoztheking is not meant to represent one genre, one album or one version of the artist. It is the continuing record of his growth.

That creative expansion is also moving beyond music. Youngoztheking currently has two upcoming books in development, extending his storytelling into long-form writing. The books represent another part of the same larger vision: building a body of work across music, visuals and literature while giving audiences more ways to experience the ideas, lessons and stories behind the artist.
